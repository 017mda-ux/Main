Hi, I'm the writer behind 151.

Investor. Analyst. Chronic overthinker of market structure.

𝕏 Twitter in LinkedIn 📧 Email Me

I started 151 because most financial commentary falls into one of two traps: it's either too dry to finish reading, or too shallow to be useful. I wanted something in between — the kind of writing you'd get if a Bloomberg analyst and a curious generalist had a conversation and actually wrote it down.

The name? 151 is Mew's Pokédex number — the rarest, most mythical Pokémon. The one that contains the DNA of every other Pokémon. The one almost nobody ever caught. The Ancient Mew card is one of the rarest cards ever printed. That felt right for what this is: rare, original thinking that most people never find. We're trying to do something most people don't: think clearly about complex systems and find the edges nobody else sees.

What I actually do

I've been investing seriously for five-plus years. I started with index funds like most sensible people. Then I got curious about individual stocks, then macro, then alternatives. Now I spend a lot of time thinking about the intersection of policy, capital markets, and the structural changes reshaping the global economy.

My day-to-day involves reading SEC filings, central bank statements, earnings transcripts, and more news than is probably healthy. I use AI tools — Claude, Perplexity, others — as research assistants to process more information than any single person could handle alone. That's the AI-first part. I'm not just talking about AI as a theme. I use it in my workflow, and I'll tell you how.

My investing philosophy

"Be roughly right rather than precisely wrong. Most people in markets are precisely wrong with a lot of confidence."

I'd describe my approach as:

  • Macro-aware, bottom-up driven. I care about the macro backdrop, but I invest in companies (and sometimes currencies, commodities, and rates). The macro is the river; the stock pick is the boat. Both matter.
  • Variant view seeking. If everyone already knows the thesis, the trade is already crowded. The interesting money is made on the disagreements between you and the consensus — and being right about them.
  • Long-termist but not passive. I'm not a trader, but I'm also not going to hold something forever out of stubbornness. Circumstances change. Theses get invalidated. The discipline is knowing the difference.
  • Intellectually honest. I will tell you when I'm wrong. I will update my views publicly. The worst thing in investing is certainty — it makes you blind to new information.

Why I write

Writing forces clarity. There's a famous quote (often misattributed) that goes: "I would have written a shorter letter, but I did not have the time." The same is true for investment analysis. It's easy to have a vague, half-formed bull case in your head. It's much harder to write it out and have it make sense. This blog is, in part, my personal forcing function.

But I also genuinely think the quality of financial analysis available to retail investors is shockingly bad. Either it's paywalled behind institutional research that costs $50,000/year, or it's free-but-useless hot takes on Twitter. There's a gap in the middle for serious, readable, independent analysis. That's what I'm trying to fill.

The AI-first part

I'm building this blog to be a next-generation research publication, not a traditional newsletter or blog. That means:

  • Using AI tools to synthesize large amounts of data quickly
  • Eventually offering AI-powered search across all my research
  • Building premium features like an AI chat over my model portfolio and thesis library
  • Writing transparently about how AI is changing investing — because it is, fast

The goal is for this to be the research publication I wish had existed when I started. Rigorous. Independent. Readable. And honest about uncertainty.
