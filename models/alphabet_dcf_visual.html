Alphabet DCF, step by step

Your case, in $ billions. A DCF asks one question: what are all of Alphabet's future cash flows worth in today's money? Tap any bar for its number.

1

Sales grow, mostly from Cloud

Revenue goes from $492B in 2026 to $1,050B in 2031. Search grows +7% a year, then +5%. Cloud grows almost fivefold.

2

Profit grows, but slower than sales

Operating profit rises from $201B to $344B. Search profit stays flat at about $135–139B because its margin falls from 52% to 40%. Cloud, at a 40% margin, supplies almost all of the growth. The orange bars below zero are company-wide costs (AI research, Other Bets).

3

Cash in vs cash spent on data centers

Cash the business makes = profit after tax + depreciation added back. Capex is spent on top. The gap is free cash flow, the cash left for owners. It is negative in 2027–28, about zero in 2029, and positive from 2030.

4

Future dollars are worth less today

You need an 8.2% return a year (k, the return the market price implies on consensus). So $1 received in 2031 is worth only 67¢ today. Each year's cash flow is shrunk by that factor.

Add the five years up and they're worth only $15B today. The heavy spending eats almost all of the near-term cash.

5

7

Move k and g yourself

Terminal value = long-run cash flow × (1 + g) ÷ (k − g). k is your required return, g is growth forever. Drag either one and watch the value change.

8

You vs consensus

Start at consensus ($286 a share, about today's price) and switch in your assumptions one at a time. Per share.

You are more cautious on Search: its margin falls to 40% vs 56% for consensus, and it grows 7% then 5% vs 10% then 8%. That costs about $83. You are more optimistic on capex: it eases to 25% of revenue after 2031, while consensus keeps it near 38%. That adds about $188. Cloud revenue is about the same.
